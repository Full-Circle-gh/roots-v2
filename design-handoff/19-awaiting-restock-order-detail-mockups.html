Naming the item an awaiting-restock order is parked on

Order drawer · 5 options · rail and topbar cropped

Order TC000518 sits in Awaiting restock and the drawer never says which of its seven products is holding it. Staff open the order, read every line, and still have to guess, or go to Products and check stock one SKU at a time.

The answer is already in the payload. awaiting_restock_pids is written at checkout when the customer chooses to wait, and reaches the drawer through GET /api/retail-admin/orders/:id, which selects o.*. Five ways to spend it.

Two colours carry the whole set. Amber says a person is needed. Dusk with a breathing dot says tacet is handling it — the order releases itself when stock lands, and that is the part the operator most needs to know.

How much to bring in, and whether the order is about to move on its own. A line already restocked reads as settled instead of still broken.

What it leaves

Free stock is live and the drawer is not. The numbers are true at open and stale a minute later, so they prompt a stock check rather than replace one.

A backend change. Free stock per waiting product has to be joined into the order detail response, and there is no parked-at column to date the wait from.

What it leaves

One name and a count, never a list. The cell already carries a pill, and a note-line that wraps to three lines makes the row taller than its neighbours.

What it costs

What the data already carries

A, B and C are front-end work in the orders drawer. Only D's numbers and the list note-line need a field added to a query.

FactWhere it comes fromIn the drawer
Which products parked the order awaiting_restock_pids, a JSON array of product ids written when the customer chooses to wait, and kept after release for audit Yes
Name, SKU and quantity The order's own items, matched on product id Yes
Why it parked fallback_choice is wait_for_restock. A pre-order line parks with no choice recorded, so an empty value means pre-order, not an error Yes
Free stock and units short getAvailable(store_id, product_id), not currently joined into the order detail response No — D only
How long it has waited No parked-at column exists. paid_at is the closest stand-in No — D only

One behaviour belongs in whichever option ships. The release check requires every line on the order to be coverable, not only the parked ones, so an order can stay held after its named product lands. Say it on the screen and it reads as the system working. Leave it out and it reads as a bug.
