Pop-ups

A card that greets people when they open a page — a promo, a reminder, a holiday notice. You see each one exactly as customers do.

How it's built today

  • A flat list of text rows — name, a run of "pages · trigger · frequency", a toggle, an Edit button. You can't see the pop-up itself.
  • Edit opens a drawer where every field is open at once: name, on/off, dismissible, trigger, delay, frequency, start & end dates, image, title, body, button label, button destination — one long scroll.
  • A small preview sits to the side, but the row list gives no picture, so you judge a visual by reading a sentence.

What that costs

  • You approve a design you never see until it's live on a customer's phone.
  • Twelve controls compete for attention; the message — the only thing that matters — has no more weight than the delay-in-seconds field.
  • "pages · trigger · frequency" reads like config, not like a plain sentence about who sees what, when.

What changed in A Recommended

  • The list becomes a gallery. Every pop-up is drawn on a phone, exactly as customers meet it — you review a picture, not a sentence of settings.
  • The message leads the editor. Headline, message, and picture are the only fields open. Everything else — where, when, how often, runs, button — folds into five quiet rows you open only to change.
  • Config reads as English. "On the Home page · shows once · opens Shop" instead of "home · on_load · once".
  • Off pop-ups grey out but stay visible, so a good design you paused is one tap from returning — not lost in a list.
  • New: Duplicate to spin a seasonal variant, and Preview on my phone before it goes live.

What changed in B

  • Least disruptive. The list you have stays a list — but each row grows a real thumbnail so a pop-up is never invisible.
  • A sticky rail previews the selected row and exposes the three fields you touch most (headline, where, how often). The 12-field drawer only opens for the rare deep edit.
  • Meta reads plainer: "Home · once · opens Shop" as a quiet caption, not raw keys.
  • Good if you keep many pop-ups and scan by name. Weaker than A at judging the actual design — the preview is small and one-at-a-time.

What changed in C

  • One quiet column, grouped by state — Live, Scheduled, Resting — so the page answers "what are customers seeing right now?" at a glance.
  • Compose is a single bar that expands in place into the same staged form as A.
  • Smallest footprint of the three; the thumbnail shrinks to a swatch and the headline shows in quotes.
  • Best when pop-ups are few and occasional. Weakest at comparing designs side by side — that's what A's gallery is for.
