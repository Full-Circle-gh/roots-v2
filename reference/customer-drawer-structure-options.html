Customer drawer — three structures

The drawer is not badly ordered so much as un-ranked: thirteen blocks, six of them identical white cards, and the two you need least are the two that take the most height. Each option below is a different answer to that. Same data, same strings, same components throughout — only structure changes.

13
blocks in the drawer

Header, snapshot, six cards, five folds. Nothing tells the eye which of the six cards matters.

~210px
Notes & tags, empty

Tag row, add field, textarea and a Save foot — the drawer's tallest card, spent on a section that is usually blank.

~170px
Device & account

Build string, sessions, openid. Support material, read when something is wrong, sized like a headline.

6×
competing borders

Six 1px cards at 24px padding on Paper. The chrome costs more height than several of the sections it wraps.

A · Rules, not cards

one column drawer height —

The card wall comes out. Sections are separated by a hairline and a kicker, the way the folds below them already are, so the whole drawer reads as one document instead of a stack of boxes. Tags and the note move into the header — that is what you know about the person, so it belongs beside their name, and it costs one line instead of a card. Top items moves to third, right under Activity. Device & account becomes a fold with the other support material. Most Tacet of the three: when in doubt, remove something.

B · Three tabs

shortest drawer height —

Header and snapshot always visible; everything else behind three tabs. Who they are is what you land on: Activity, Top items, Addresses. History holds the four ledgers. Account holds device, sessions, openid, diagnostics, affiliate. Same header treatment for tags and the note. Cuts the drawer to roughly a screen with no scrolling — the cost is that a ledger is now two clicks away, and a tab hides whether anything is inside it until you open it.

C · Two columns

nothing hidden drawer height —

The drawer widens and the body splits: left is the person's trade — Activity, Top items, Orders by month — and right is the file on them — Addresses, the compact note, Device & account. Cards stay here, because with two columns a border is what tells you which column a block belongs to; they drop to 20px padding. Notes & tags keeps a card but loses the permanent textarea: it shows the note and opens to edit. Nothing is hidden behind a click and the height roughly halves. The cost is width — this needs a wider drawer than ships today, and it collapses back to one column on a narrow screen.

Common to all three. Tags and the note stop being a 210px card. Top items moves above Addresses and the chart. The chart drops from 96px of bars to 52px — twelve months of shape does not need a third of a screen. Device & account is demoted everywhere: to a fold in A and B, to the bottom of the quiet column in C.

The note's edit affordance is the thing to decide. All three show the note as a line and open an editor on Edit. That is the whole space saving. If the team types into that field constantly, the saving is wrong and the textarea should stay — worth checking how many customers actually carry a note before building it.

No new strings. Every label and every Chinese pair here is lifted from the live drawer in public/retail-admin/08-customers.js, except three that are restructures of existing ones and are proposals, not locked: the tab labels in B and the "+ tag" chip. Nothing has been written to docs/tacet-delivery-vocabulary.md because nothing here is built yet; if B is chosen, its three tab labels go in as composed strings in the same commit as the code.

Zero Dusk in all three drawers, as today. The active tab in B is an Ink underline — an operator navigating is not an automation.
