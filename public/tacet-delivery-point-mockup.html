The one thing with two options

The marker means two different things — approximate and untouched, and dragged and confirmed. If those read alike, an operator confirms without looking and the feature buys nothing. Two treatments, both live above:

A · the area collapses into a point. Untouched is an area: a wide dashed circle around a hollow centre, which is the honest picture of a district-level match. Touched is a point: the circle is gone and a solid pin stands on one spot. Shape, size and the presence of an area all change; no colour carries the difference. Confirming without dragging stays possible in one tap, which is what §5 and D1 want when the guess happens to be right.

B · inert until moved. Untouched is a hollow crosshair that nudges on a slow loop, and the primary button is disabled and says Drag the point to confirm. Touching the marker fills it solid and arms the button. The distinction is enforced rather than shown — you cannot confirm a point you have not put your hand on — at the cost of a tap on every order, including the ones already sitting on the right building, and a 1px nudge still satisfies it.

The trade is engagement against friction. A trusts the operator and makes the two states unmistakable; B removes the trust and makes the tap mandatory. Both are built — pick on the floor, not from this page.

What is not real here

No endpoint, no queue row, no CONFIRM_POINT_SQL, no fetch. Every number, address and date is typed into the markup. The map is a drawing — no tiles, no provider, no key. Buttons move this page's own state and nothing else.
